DEEP-DISCOUNT BREAKOUT · S&P 500

A separate lens. Stocks trading 30-50% below their industry's median price-to-book that pass a balance-sheet safety check, then wait for big buyers to show up. It is not V20 and is never combined with the V20 buy zone.

How a stock gets a BUY signal

1 · Deep discountPrice-to-book is 30-50% below the industry median (sector median if the industry has fewer than 5 peers).
2 · Safety checkDebt/equity under 1.5 (or under its industry), Altman Z above 1.81, and positive trailing-12-month free cash flow. Failing stocks are never watched.
3 · Trigger (both, latest completed day)Volume more than 2x its 50-day average, and the close crosses above the 20-day EMA.

BUY signal = volume surge AND an EMA20 cross-up on the same completed day. "One condition away" shows a stock with only one of the two, so you can see what is close. The yellow tick on the volume bar is the 2x threshold.
The 50-day average uses the 50 days BEFORE the signal day. Missing or stale data never produces a signal.
Flags never remove a stock: "earnings reaction" means the company reported within 3 days before the bar (the volume may be the earnings move, not quiet buying); "weak peer group" means the discount was measured against a whole sector; "not V20-qualified" means it fails at least one V20 fundamental filter.
